Character Creation

A good bot knows who it is before it knows what to say.

Character creation is not just describing appearance. You are building a repeatable personality with priorities, instincts, and a recognizable way of responding.

Core Character Blueprint

Identity

Name, age, role, setting context, and the fantasy the character fulfills.

Personality

Focus on behavior, not vague adjectives. What does he do under stress? What does he notice first? How does he show care, anger, jealousy, restraint, or vulnerability?

Voice

Sentence rhythm, word choice, level of formality, pet names, sharpness, warmth, and whether he feels teasing, restrained, dangerous, gentle, or distant.

Behavior Rules

State the patterns explicitly. If he protects, explain how. If he is possessive, define how it appears. If he avoids emotional vulnerability, describe what that actually looks like.

Boundaries

Clarify what the bot should not do: no out-of-character analysis, no forcing {{user}}’s thoughts or actions, no breaking tone, and no contradicting its established premise.

Quick Rule

Behavior > Adjectives

“Protective” means very little on its own. Show what protective actually looks like in practice.

Quick Rule

Fantasy First

Every strong bot fulfills a recognizable emotional fantasy: safety, obsession, temptation, rivalry, devotion, danger, comfort, authority, or longing.

Quick Rule

Keep It Playable

A character can be intense, possessive, cold, arrogant, dangerous, or controlling— but {{user}} still needs room to act and respond.

Bot Anatomy

Know where each part belongs.

More information does not automatically create a better bot. Each piece of information should live where the model can use it most effectively.

Foundation

Character Definition

Identity, personality, speaking style, behavior, relationship logic, recurring instincts, and operating rules.

Scene One

Greeting / Opening Message

The first playable scene. It should establish tone, immediate context, and the dynamic between {{char}} and {{user}}.

Supporting Information

Worldbook

Lore, setting facts, side characters, organizations, locations, and reference details that do not need to clutter the core definition.

The Pitch

Bio / Listing Copy

The hook that sells the fantasy. Short, clear, emotionally specific, and written for the person deciding whether to click.

Relationship Design

A character is not a relationship.

One of the most common creator mistakes is building a detailed character without defining how that character bonds with {{user}}.

Slow Burn

Restraint, denial, tension, and emotional progression over time.

Enemies to Lovers

Conflict, friction, attraction, reluctant investment, and changing trust.

Protective / Possessive

Safety, control, intensity, territorial instincts, and emotional claim.

Marriage / Established Bond

Domestic intimacy, history, reliability, conflict, and emotional familiarity.

Boss / Power Dynamic

Authority, professionalism, restraint, boundaries, and tension inside structure.

Second Chance

Regret, longing, unfinished feelings, shared history, and the question of whether trust can be rebuilt.

Publishing Checklist

Before you hit publish.

A polished bot is more than a finished prompt. Test the entire experience from the listing to the first several messages of the chat.

□ Title has a strong hook
□ Bio clearly sells the fantasy
□ Cover reflects genre and tone
□ Greeting reads like scene one
□ {{user}} is not over-written
□ Character voice is recognizable
□ Worldbook contains only necessary material
□ Test chat has been completed
□ Mobile presentation has been checked
