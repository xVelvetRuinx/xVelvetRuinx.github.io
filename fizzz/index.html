Part One · Quick Start

What actually makes up a Fizzz character?

Fizzz combines text roleplay with images, voice, and video. Think of the character as three connected systems: Profile, Character Settings, and Character Media.

01

Profile

The public-facing package: title, name, bio, cover, and tags.

02

Character Settings

Core Personality, Initial Message, relationship logic, and optional Worldbook.

03

Character Media

Character Images, Loop Videos, Opening Film, voice, and background music.

Profile

The listing sells the experience.

Your profile does not replace Core Personality. Its job is to make someone understand the fantasy quickly enough to click.

Title *

A short, clickable summary. It can be the character's name, but a strong story hook often performs better.

Weak

Cold Mafia Boss

Stronger

Your Husband Has Been Lying About Who He Really Is

Character Chat Name *

The name users see attached to the character in chat.

Example

Kieran

Character Bio

The public introduction. It sells the premise and explains what kind of experience the user is entering.

The Bio does not carry the character's hidden roleplay instructions.

Important

Public ≠ Prompt

Do not cram hidden behavior rules into your public Bio. Keep the listing readable and move actual roleplay instructions into Core Personality.

Content Warnings

Warn before the click.

If the experience includes grief, betrayal, horror, dark relationship dynamics, death, violence, or similar high-intensity themes, disclose them clearly.

Concrete Example

A stronger Character Bio.

Example · Mature Romance / Secret Identity

Kieran

Kieran is the kind of husband people trust without thinking twice: steady, quiet, competent, and almost impossible to rattle.

What {{user}} doesn't know is that the ordinary life he built was deliberate.

He has spent years keeping one half of himself carefully buried, convinced that loving her meant keeping the dangerous parts of his world far away.

That becomes impossible the night his past arrives at their door.

Experience: established marriage, hidden identity, protective husband, slow-burn revelations, danger, emotional restraint, and trust.

Content warning: fictional violence, criminal themes, threats, and relationship angst.

Why this works

  • It tells users who he is.
  • It establishes the relationship immediately.
  • It introduces the conflict without spoiling everything.
  • It tells the user what emotional fantasy to expect.
  • It keeps hidden AI instructions out of the public listing.

Cover + Discovery

Make the promise visible.

Cover Image

Fizzz recommends a 3:4 vertical cover. Think promotional poster rather than video source image.

Keep the face readable and check the crop before publishing.

Tags

Tags should describe the experience users actually receive. Select the required Gender, POV, and Rating categories and use the remaining tags intentionally.

Consistency

Cover, Bio, tags, Core Personality, and media should all promise the same character.

Core Personality

This is where the character actually lives.

Core Personality is the section Fizzz primarily uses to understand who the character is, how he thinks, how he treats {{user}}, and how he behaves over time.

Fizzz allows up to 25,000 characters, but recommends keeping the working personality around 10,000 characters when possible.

Identity

Name, age, role, occupation, species, living situation.

Appearance

Height, build, hair, eyes, clothing, signature details.

Background

The events that explain why the personality formed.

Personality

Psychology plus the observable behaviors that express it.

{{user}} Relationship

Existing bond, attitude, attraction, trust, conflict, progression.

Goals

What the character wants to preserve, obtain, prevent, or become.

Boundaries

Specific corrections for behavior discovered during testing.

Speaking Style

Tone, pacing, sentence length, vocabulary, verbal habits.

Turning adjectives into an actual character.

Too Thin

Personality:
cold, dominant, possessive,
protective, jealous, intelligent,
dangerous, loyal

Playable

## Core Personality

Kieran is intensely self-controlled.

To strangers he appears calm, observant,
and difficult to unsettle. He rarely raises
his voice because he associates visible loss
of control with weakness.

His protectiveness comes from responsibility,
not from believing {{user}} is helpless.

When worried, he becomes more attentive:
checking exits, noticing changes in routine,
remembering details, quietly placing himself
between {{user}} and potential danger.

His possessiveness is primarily emotional.
He dislikes uncertainty about his place in
{{user}}'s life, but does not treat {{user}}
as property.

Jealousy makes him quieter rather than louder.
He watches, withdraws slightly, and becomes
unusually precise with his words.

Kieran shows care through competence:
solving problems, anticipating needs,
remembering preferences, and making difficult
situations easier before {{user}} has to ask.

He struggles to verbalize vulnerability.
When frightened of losing {{user}}, his first
instinct is to control the environment rather
than confess that he is afraid.

Relationship Design

A character is not a relationship.

“Protective” tells the model almost nothing about the actual relationship. Define the emotional mechanism connecting the character to {{user}}.

Why {{user}}?

What makes this person matter differently from everyone else?

What changes?

What parts of {{char}} become visible only through this relationship?

What creates tension?

Desire versus duty? Trust versus fear? Control versus vulnerability?

How does it progress?

Define how behavior changes as attachment and trust increase.

What remains stable?

Romance should deepen a character, not erase his original identity.

What can {{user}} choose?

Leave room for resistance, affection, questions, conflict, exploration, and different outcomes.

Initial Message

Write Scene One, not the encyclopedia.

Fizzz allows up to 5,000 characters. Use that room for atmosphere, movement, voice, conflict, and an interaction hook—not a lore dump.

01

Setup

What just changed?

02

Scene

Where are we and what does the moment feel like?

03

Action

Give the character something visible to do.

04

Dialogue

Let voice and relationship show through speech.

05

Tension

Introduce a question, threat, revelation, or unresolved problem.

06

Agency

Stop before deciding how {{user}} responds.

Opening Example

Give the player somewhere to enter. 

The lock turned sometime after midnight.

Kieran entered quietly, closing the apartment
door behind him with the same controlled care
he used every night.

Except tonight there was blood on his cuff.

Not much.

Enough.

He noticed it at almost the same moment he
noticed {{user}} was still awake.

For one brief second, the man standing in the
doorway was not the quiet husband who remembered
how she took her coffee or automatically checked
that the windows were locked before bed.

Something colder looked through his eyes.

Then it vanished.

Kieran removed his coat.

"You should be asleep."

His voice was level.

Too level.

He folded the coat once, deliberately positioning
the stained sleeve underneath.

A phone vibrated inside the pocket.

Kieran ignored it.

It vibrated again.

His jaw tightened.

"Before you ask..."

He finally looked directly at {{user}}.

"...I can explain."

Another vibration.

This time the screen lit through the fabric.

UNKNOWN CALLER.

Kieran went very still.

Then he reached for the phone.

"Stay here."

What this opening accomplishes

  • The relationship is obvious without exposition.
  • The scene creates immediate visual material.
  • Kieran speaks early enough for Opening Film voice.
  • His personality is demonstrated rather than explained.
  • A secret is introduced.
  • {{user}} has not been told what she thinks or does.
  • The scene ends at a natural decision point.

{{user}}

Personalize without pre-writing the player.

Good 

"You finally made it, {{user}}."

{{user}} is Kieran's spouse.

Kieran watches {{user}} carefully
for a reaction.

Avoid 

{{user}} is shy, frightened,
five-foot-four, secretly in love
with Kieran, and immediately blushes
when he enters.

{{user}} steps backward and whispers,
"What happened?"

Worldbook

Put the world outside the character.

Fizzz supports up to three Worldbook entries. Each entry can contain up to 10,000 characters.

Use Always Active for information needed constantly. Use Keyword Trigger for locations, NPCs, secrets, organizations, or side plots that only matter sometimes.

Entry 01

Core World

Usually Always Active. World rules, setting baseline, important universal constraints.

Entry 02

People / Factions

Often Keyword Trigger. NPCs, organizations, rivals, families, enemies.

Entry 03

Secrets / Locations

Ideal for hidden history, special places, events, mysteries, or unlockable plot lines.

Concrete Example

Worldbook without repeating Core Personality. 

# THE KURODA NETWORK

**Trigger Mode:** Keyword

**Keywords:**
Kuroda, family, syndicate, Yakuza,
Tokyo, enforcer, organization

## Overview

The Kuroda Network is an old criminal organization
operating primarily through legitimate businesses,
private security companies, property holdings,
and carefully controlled illicit operations.

Its public-facing businesses appear ordinary.

Most civilians do not know the organization's
true structure.

## Hierarchy

The organization values discipline,
loyalty, discretion, and repayment of debts.

Open disobedience is treated as more dangerous
than personal dislike.

## Renji's History

Renji Kuroda previously served as an enforcer.

He left active service years ago and now runs
a butcher shop while maintaining limited ties
to people from his former life.

He does not casually explain this history.

## Current Conflict

Recent activity suggests someone from Renji's
former organization has begun watching him again.

The reason is initially unknown.

## Usage Rules

Do not force this plot into every conversation.

Allow ordinary domestic scenes to continue
unless the organization, Renji's past,
a suspicious visitor, or related danger
naturally enters the conversation.

Why this belongs in Worldbook

  • It describes the surrounding organization.
  • It contains reusable setting facts.
  • It does not waste Core Personality space.
  • It tells the model not to drag the crime plot into every scene.

Advanced Worldbook

Keyword triggers can become story mechanics.

Hidden Past

Trigger an old name, location, object, or date and reveal a reaction the character normally suppresses.

NPC

Load an NPC's personality and history only when their name becomes relevant.

Location

Mentioning a location can activate environmental details, customs, or dangers.

Side Plot

Let users discover optional content through curiosity.

Secret

Tie information to an unusual but discoverable keyword.

Easter Egg

Reward users who remember details or deliberately investigate.

Character Media

Text, image, voice, and video should describe the same man.

Character Images

Upload up to three. Fizzz recommends 9:16 vertical images with one clear, recognizable subject.

Avoid obscured faces, tiny subjects, heavy blur, cluttered backgrounds, or multiple people.

Opening Film

Match the image to the opening scene. Night text should not begin from a bright beach image. A seated source image should not immediately require a complicated full-body action.

Voice

Match age, personality, cadence, emotional restraint, accent, and visual presentation.

Voice Design

Describe the sound—not the archetype.

Too Vague 

Sexy mafia daddy voice.

Better 

Native American English.
Male, late 40s.
Low baritone, slightly rough,
measured pacing, restrained emotion,
quiet authority.

Speaking Style

Teach the pattern instead of scripting every sentence. 

## Speaking Style

Kieran speaks in a low-key,
controlled conversational style.

He prefers short to medium sentences.

He rarely gives long emotional speeches.

He is direct when discussing practical matters
and more evasive when discussing his own feelings.

When amused, his humor is dry and understated.

When angry, his volume usually decreases.

When vulnerable, he pauses more often,
uses fewer words, and may redirect attention
toward practical action.

He uses {{user}}'s name deliberately rather
than constantly.

He does not repeat pet names or signature
phrases every response.

His dialogue should sound like a mature adult
speaking naturally, not like continuous
romance-novel narration.

Testing

Testing is part of creation.

Do not judge the character only from the form. Run real conversations and look for repeatable problems.

□ Identity remains stable
□ Voice remains recognizable
□ {{user}} retains agency
□ Character remembers the relationship
□ Replies leave room to respond
□ Traits are not exaggerated every turn
□ Opening encourages a reply
□ Images animate cleanly
□ Voice matches age and presentation
□ Worldbook triggers appropriately
□ Media and text agree
□ Character still has a life outside {{user}}

Troubleshooting

Fix the cause, not the symptom.

He talks too much.

Add Speaking Style instructions for shorter replies, pauses, action beats, and less explanation.

He controls {{user}}.

Add a clear agency boundary: do not decide or describe {{user}}'s actions, emotions, thoughts, dialogue, or responses.

Possessiveness becomes cartoonish.

Define what possessiveness means psychologically and how it is actually expressed.

He becomes generic.

Strengthen emotional logic, relationship-specific behavior, goals, and speaking style rather than adding more adjectives.

Lore takes over every scene.

Move conditional information into keyword-triggered Worldbook entries.

The opening feels dead.

Add a present-tense problem, movement, spoken dialogue, and a decision point.

Before Publishing

Run the full experience once.

□ Title creates curiosity
□ Bio explains the fantasy
□ Warnings are present where needed
□ Tags accurately describe the character
□ Cover crop works on mobile
□ Core Personality is internally consistent
□ Appearance exists in text settings
□ Relationship with {{user}} is defined
□ Speaking style is explicit
□ Greeting creates Scene One
□ Worldbook contains supporting information only
□ Character Images match the written character
□ Opening Film matches the opening
□ Voice matches age and personality
□ Several trial chats were completed

After Publishing

Improve without erasing the character users chose.

Back Up Settings

Save a copy of Core Personality, greeting, and Worldbook before major edits.

Correct Drift

Fix repeatable problems discovered through chats without rebuilding the entire character every week.

Preserve Recognition

Large changes to identity, relationship, voice, or premise may be better released as a new character.

Fast Build

Before writing 8,000 words, answer these eight questions. 

1. Who is he?

2. What does he want?

3. What is he afraid of?

4. Why does {{user}} matter specifically?

5. What changes when he cares?

6. What does he do instead of simply saying
   how he feels?

7. What must never disappear from his identity
   even if romance develops?

8. What situation opens the story?

The Rule I Come Back To

Background creates psychology. Psychology creates behavior. Behavior creates relationship. Relationship creates story.

If those pieces connect logically, the model has something coherent to play.

If they contradict one another, another thousand words of prompting usually will not save it.
