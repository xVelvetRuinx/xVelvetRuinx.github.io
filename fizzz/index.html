Character Creation

A good bot knows who it is before it knows what to say.

Character creation is not just describing appearance. You are building a repeatable personality with priorities, instincts, and a recognizable way of responding.

Core Character Blueprint

Identity

Name, age, role, setting context, and the fantasy the character fulfills.

Personality

Focus on behavior, not vague adjectives. What does he do under stress? What does he notice first? How does he show care, anger, jealousy, restraint?

Voice

Sentence rhythm, word choice, level of formality, pet names, sharpness, warmth, and whether he feels teasing, restrained, dangerous, or soft.

Behavior Rules

State the patterns explicitly. If he protects, say how. If he is possessive, say how it shows. If he avoids emotional vulnerability, define what that looks like.

Boundaries

Clarify what the bot should not do: no out-of-character analysis, no forcing {{user}}’s thoughts, no breaking tone, no contradicting its own premise.

Quick Rule

Behavior > Adjectives

“Protective” means very little on its own. Show what protective looks like in practice.

Quick Rule

Fantasy First

Every strong bot fulfills a clear emotional fantasy: safety, obsession, temptation, rivalry, devotion, danger, comfort, authority, longing.

Quick Rule

Keep It Playable

A character can be intense, possessive, cold, arrogant, or dangerous— but the user still needs room to act and respond.

Bot Anatomy

Know where each part belongs.

Character Definition

Identity, personality, speaking style, behavior, relationship logic, recurring instincts, and operating rules.

Greeting / Opening Message

The first scene. It should establish tone, immediate context, and the dynamic between {{char}} and {{user}}.

Worldbook

Lore, setting facts, side characters, and reference details that do not need to clutter the main definition.

Bio / Listing Copy

The hook that sells the fantasy. Short, clean, and emotionally clear.

Relationship Design

A character is not a relationship.

One of the most common creator mistakes is building a detailed character without defining how that character bonds with {{user}}.

Slow Burn

Restraint, denial, tension, and emotional progression over time.

Enemies to Lovers

Conflict, friction, attraction, and reluctant investment.

Protective / Possessive

Safety, control, intensity, territorial instincts, and emotional claim.

Marriage / Established Bond

Domestic intimacy, history, reliability, and emotional familiarity.

Boss / Power Dynamic

Authority, professionalism, restraint, and tension inside structure.

Second Chance

Regret, longing, unfinished feelings, and emotional history.

Publishing Checklist

Before you hit publish.

□ Title has a strong hook
□ Bio clearly sells the fantasy
□ Cover reflects genre and tone
□ Greeting reads like scene one
□ {{user}} is not over-written
□ Character voice is recognizable
□ Worldbook only contains necessary material
□ Test chat has been completed
□ Mobile layout has been checked
