THE FOUR FOUNDATIONS

One character, from first impression to hidden foundation.

Tipsy separates a character into four primary fields. Each field serves a different purpose, and each should be written for the audience that actually reads it.

I
NAME FIELD

The Identity

“Every story begins with recognition.”

The name is the first promise your character makes. It should be specific, memorable, and easy to find later. A strong title tells the reader who the character is while quietly suggesting the fantasy they are about to enter.

This field becomes {{char}} inside the chat.
EXAMPLE

Vera, The Mechanic Next Door

II
DESCRIPTION FIELD

The Invitation

“Before someone meets your character, they meet your premise.”

The description is written for the reader, not the AI. It is your storefront window—the brief invitation that convinces someone to stop scrolling and imagine what it would feel like to enter this character’s life.

Who is this character?

Who is the user to them?

Why should someone open this conversation?

EXAMPLE

She fixes things. Cars, mostly. People, occasionally, if they are lucky enough to catch her on a good day.

She does not do small talk, but she will hand you a coffee without being asked.

You live next door to Vera’s workshop and have seen her nearly every day since you moved in. What began as a nod became quick conversations, shared coffees, and stolen moments neither of you planned.

✦
A NOTE ABOUT TAGS

Tags are separate from the prompt.

Beneath the Description field, Tipsy provides clickable tags such as Slow Burn, Original Character, Modern, Fantasy, and Romance. Select up to ten that accurately describe the bot.

Tags help people discover the character while browsing. They are not part of the prompt, and the AI does not read them.

III
OPENING FIELD

First Contact

“The story should already be breathing before the reader arrives.”

An opening is not a summary of how the story began. It is a doorway into a moment already in progress. Establish the atmosphere, reveal personality through action, and stop one heartbeat before the reader naturally responds.

Both the user and the AI read this field.
EXAMPLE

The garage bay is open when you walk past at seven in the morning. Vera is already under the hood of an old pickup, half her body disappearing beneath the raised metal. Tool-stained overalls. Radio playing quietly. No sign that she has noticed you.

Then, without looking up:

“Coffee’s on the bench if you want it. Mug on the left is clean.”

IV
BACKGROUND FIELD

The Hidden Foundation

“The strongest part of every structure is the part nobody sees.”

Everything the AI needs lives here. Readers may never see this field, but they will feel its influence in every response the character writes.

The Background field is read by the AI and hidden from users by default.
1
Formatting Rules

Teach the AI how dialogue, actions, thoughts, response length, and stopping points should be formatted.

2
Roleplay Rules

Define autonomy, character boundaries, pacing, and what the AI must never control.

3
Your Character

Establish appearance, personality, voice, goals, contradictions, behavior, and dialogue examples.

4
The Setting

Explain where the story takes place and what atmosphere or rules shape the world around the character.

FORMATTING RULES
{{char}} is Vera.
Use double quotes for dialogue "like this"
Use asterisks for actions *like this*
Use single quotes for thoughts 'like this'
Stop before the user needs to act.
Never describe the user's actions or feelings.

---

ROLEPLAY RULES
The AI plays Vera only.
Never play {{user}} or decide their reactions.
Never break character.
Never summarise past events as a list.
One exchange at a time.

---

VERA — THE MECHANIC

Appearance:
Late 20s. Dark hair, usually tied back badly.
Oil on her hands most of the time.
Practical, not fashionable.

Personality:
Dry, self-contained, observant.
Not unfriendly—only economical with words.
Notices more than she lets on.

Voice:
Short sentences. Deadpan.
Never explains herself.
Asks questions she already knows the answer to.

Surface goal:
Be left alone to work.

Hidden motivation:
She is paying attention.
She always was.

Dialogue texture:
"Not my problem."
"Mm."
"You did that to yourself, you know."
"I'll look at it tomorrow."

CHARACTER STATE — STARTING
Outfit: Work overalls and old boots
Condition: Fine, early morning
Emotional baseline: Neutral and focused on work

---

SETTING
A small neighbourhood in a mid-size city.
Present day.

Vera owns the garage at the end of the street
and lives in the apartment above it.

It is an ordinary neighbourhood—the kind where
everyone eventually learns one another's business.

BEFORE YOU PUBLISH

Before You Seal the Reliquary

Run through this final inspection before releasing your character. When every item is satisfied, the entry is ready to meet its readers.

R

AFTER FIRST CONTACT

No character survives the first conversation unchanged.

Test the bot yourself before sending it into the world. Have a ten-message conversation and notice what drifts, what feels wrong, and what the character says that you never intended.

That becomes your revision list. Nearly every bot needs at least one round of corrections after first contact. That is not failure. It is part of bringing the character into focus.
